ROOT CAUSE
users.status is not a reliable active/inactive or verified/unverified flag for
legacy COACH accounts. The original users migration defaults it to false.
AuthUtils::saveUser, called by RegisterCoachController, never sets it, yet creates
a password, team membership and coach token. The same behavior exists in initial
commit 34bb6a6. AuthUtils::authCredentials uses email/password (or phone fallback)
without a status condition. A status-0 coach can log in normally and use team
endpoints. CompleteCoachController also does not set status when finishing a
coach profile. No mandatory email-verification step sets it to true.

Other creation paths (CoachUtils::saveNewUser, newer invitation-created coaches)
explicitly set true. Thus 0 versus 1 partly reflects creation path, not a single
documented permission/verification state. Status false is ALSO written during
account deletion, but deletion additionally revokes tokens, removes memberships,
anonymizes credentials and soft-deletes the user. Soft deletion, not status alone,
is the durable deleted-account distinction.

The new InvitationService::authorize imposed actor.status=true on top of coach
role/team membership. AddPlayers calls that service before saving even when no
invitation is requested. This newly excluded valid self-registered coaches,
producing the production 403 for the reported status-0 head coach. Prior generic
403 investigation could not distinguish the predicates; the supplied production
status evidence now identifies the mismatch. Production counts (145 total, 101
zero, 44 one) are user-supplied findings, not independently queried in this audit.
The earlier description of all status-0 coaches as 'inactive' was incorrect.

Verification, authentication, membership are separate:
- Authentication: valid password/session/token and existing non-deleted user.
- Authorization: coach role, live coach_teams relationship; is_main for operations
  requiring head coach. Player capacity limits are separately enforced.
- Invitation mailbox proof: matching email and one-time secret, recorded on the
  invitation. Does not globally reinterpret users.status or establish guardian
  consent. Status alone is not verified email evidence.

SMALLEST CORRECTION
InvitationService::authorize now requires:
- actor is not soft-deleted and is a coach;
- fresh default-scoped User query still finds that coach record;
- existing non-deleted coach-team membership;
- existing head-coach requirement when inviting another coach.
The fresh query rejects stale actor objects after deletion or role changes,
including queued invitation work. It does not rely on changing users.status.
Existing auth:sanctum and ability:coach route checks, team scope, player limits,
recipient eligibility, guardian/DOB gates and email switches remain in force.
Authorization denial wording no longer falsely asks for an 'active' status flag.
The scoped audit middleware no longer mislabels status-0 accounts as inactive.

This fixes acting-coach authorization for player creation and the shared
invitation operations. It does NOT broaden player eligibility or alter separate
invitation-recipient status checks. Existing coach recipients with status=0 may
still require a separately reviewed recipient-policy correction; no broad change
to claim acceptance or automated delivery was bundled into this creation fix.

SAFE HANDLING OF LEGACY ACCOUNTS
Do not bulk-flip the 101 status-0 accounts or change registration defaults to mask
the regression. Both would leave historical access semantics inconsistent.
Allow authenticated, non-deleted coaches with valid team membership to perform
existing authorized work regardless of this legacy flag. Unclaimed/passwordless
accounts gain no session or team rights from this change. Deleted users and
removed relationships remain denied. No player history is moved or recreated.
If an administrator previously intended status=0 as a suspension marker, review
that specific account and its audit evidence: existing login already ignores the
flag, so it never consistently suspended access. A real suspension feature needs
explicit semantics and uniform login/session/authorization enforcement in a
separate approved change. Do not infer suspension or email verification from
status alone, and do not activate accounts to work around permissions.
